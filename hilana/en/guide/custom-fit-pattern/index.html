Custom-fit knitting patterns: from your measurements to the pattern

Patterns come in sizes, and hardly anyone is exactly one: the bust of one size, the length of another, the arms of a third. A custom-fit pattern starts from your measurements and your gauge, not from a size chart.

1. Take your measurements

  • Bust or chest: around the fullest part, tape level and not tight.
  • Waist and hips, if the garment is shaped.
  • Length: from the shoulder, next to the neck, to where you want the garment to end.
  • Sleeve length, and around the upper arm and the wrist.

Measure over what you would wear underneath, ideally with someone's help.

Another way: measure your favourite sweater. Lay it flat on a table and measure its chest width, its length and its sleeves. Those measurements already include the ease you like, so in that case you don't need to add any.

2. Choose the ease

Ease is how much bigger the garment is than your body, and it is added to your bust measurement. Every garment should have some: with none, it clings to the bust and hips. Some people like that, but most of us prefer a little room.

The usual reference: +5 to +10 cm (2 to 4 in) of positive ease for a sweater or a comfortable everyday garment that neither squeezes nor hangs loose. Less if you want it fitted; more if you want it roomy.

3. Turn centimetres into stitches

Your gauge swatch tells you how many stitches fit in 10 cm. The rest is simple proportion:

Example: you want the back to measure 50 cm and your gauge is 22 stitches in 10 cm.
50 × 22 ÷ 10 = 110 stitches to cast on.

Rows work the same way: the length you want times your rows in 10 cm, divided by 10.

4. Spread the increases and decreases

This is the tedious part. If your hips are wider than your waist, you have to decrease along the way and spread those decreases over the rows in between. The same goes for the armholes, the neckline, the shoulders and the sleeve cap: each section has its own sums, and they all have to agree so the pieces sew together.

You can do it by hand, with paper and patience. Or you can let an app do the maths.
