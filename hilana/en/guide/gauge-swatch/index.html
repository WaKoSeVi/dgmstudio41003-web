How to knit and measure a gauge swatch

A gauge swatch is a small square you knit before you start. It tells you how many stitches and rows fit in 10 cm (4 in) for you, with your yarn and your needles. Every number in a garment comes from it.

Why the yarn label is not enough

The label gives a guideline gauge, someone else's. Everyone knits a little tighter or looser, and the stitch pattern changes things a lot: garter or seed stitch do not measure like stockinette. If your gauge is not the pattern's, the garment comes out bigger or smaller even if you follow every line.

How to knit it

  1. Use what you will use for the garment: same yarn, same needles, same stitch.
  2. Make it bigger than 10 cm (4 in), so you can measure in the middle, away from the edges, which always come out uneven.
  3. Knit at your usual pace, the way you will knit the garment.
  4. Wash it and let it dry flat, the way you will wash the garment. Many yarns change size when washed, and what matters is how it will end up.

How to measure it

  1. Lay the swatch on a table without stretching it.
  2. Mark 10 cm (4 in) across the middle with a ruler and count the stitches inside. Count half stitches too.
  3. Do the same vertically and count the rows.
  4. Write down both numbers, the yarn and the needles. You will want them next time.
Example: 22 stitches and 30 rows in 10 cm means your gauge is "22 sts and 30 rows to 10 cm (4 in)".

If it does not match the pattern

  • More stitches than the pattern: you knit tighter. Try slightly larger needles.
  • Fewer stitches: you knit looser. Try slightly smaller needles.
  • Knit a new swatch with the new needles and measure again.

Or keep your gauge and recalculate the pattern with it. That is what the custom-fit pattern guide explains.
