Hilana privacy policy

Translation of the original in Spanish. If there is any difference, the Spanish version prevails.

Last updated: 29 October 2026.

The short version

Your measurements, your patterns and your notes never reach us: they are stored on your device and, if you have your phone's backup turned on, in that backup, which goes to your Google or iCloud account and not to ours. There are no accounts. On Android, only if you agree, the app sends Google Firebase the crashes it has and which screens are used; on the iPhone it does not include Firebase. The free version shows a Google ad before each pattern. If you buy Premium on Android, the purchase is checked with Google through a server of ours, which only receives the purchase code; on the iPhone it is checked on the phone itself.

What the app stores and where

Everything you type stays inside your device:

  • Your measurements and those of the people you knit for.
  • Your yarn swatches.
  • The patterns you save and the notes you add to them.
  • Your settings: language, units and region.

They are in the app's own database, which no other app can access. We never see them: the app does not send them anywhere. Apart from the files you create yourself (below), the only thing that takes them off the device is the phone's own backup, which is explained in the next section.

Your phone's backup

If you have your phone's backup turned on, the system stores in it the data from your apps, including Hilana's. On Android it goes to your Google account and on the iPhone, to your iCloud. That is why, when you change phones, your measurements and your patterns come back on their own.

That backup is not made by Hilana but by the system, and it is stored in your account, not in ours: we cannot see it or open it. It is stored and protected by Google or Apple, under their own terms.

If you do not want your Hilana data to be there, you can turn off the backup in the phone's settings. On Android it is usually under Settings → Google → Backup, although the name varies by manufacturer. On the iPhone, under Settings → [your name] → iCloud. If you turn it off, when you change phones you will have to move your data with the app's own backup.

What is sent, only if you agree

This is Android only. On the iPhone the app does not include Google Firebase, does not ask you anything about this and sends neither crashes nor statistics.

The first time you open the app, it asks whether you want to help improve it. If you say no, nothing is sent. If you say yes, the following is sent to Google Firebase (Google Ireland Limited), and nothing else:

  • Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics): if the app suddenly closes, which part of the code failed, the device model and the version of Android and of the app.
  • Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase): which screens are opened, at which step of the design a pattern is left, what type of garment is generated (sweater, hat…) and whether it is saved, printed or exported as a PDF. They come with a random installation identifier, not with your name or your email.

Your measurements, the names of the people, the names of the patterns and your notes are never sent. These reports do not include the device's advertising identifier and are not used for advertising. How long what is sent is kept is in Deleting your data.

You can change each of the two things separately, whenever you want, in Settings → Help improve the app. Withdrawing permission for crash reports also deletes any that were waiting to be sent, and withdrawing permission for statistics deletes the installation identifier.

The legal basis is your consent. Google processes this data as a processor under its Firebase data protection terms.

The ad in the free version

Before showing a pattern, the free version shows an ad from Google AdMob (Google Ireland Limited). To serve it, Google receives technical data from the device (the model, the version of the system, the IP address and how the ad is interacted with) and, only if you agree, the advertising identifier to choose ads based on your interests. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the app asks you with Google's form when it starts, and you can change your answer whenever you want in Settings → Ad privacy options.

On the iPhone, in addition, the advertising identifier is only used if you also allow it in the Apple prompt that appears afterwards, the one that asks whether you let Hilana track your activity. That answer is changed in the iPhone's settings, under Privacy & Security → Tracking.

The ad never receives anything you type: no measurements, no names, no notes. With Premium there is no ad.

The legal basis is your consent for personalised ads and our legitimate interest in funding the free version. Google processes this data under its own AdMob terms.

Buying Premium

The payment is handled by Google Play on Android and by the App Store on the iPhone. We never see your card, your name or your email.

On the iPhone, the App Store signs each purchase and the app checks that signature on the phone itself: nothing is sent to any server of ours.

On Android, there are programs that trick the app into believing it has been paid for. To prevent this, when you buy and from time to time afterwards (at most once every 12 hours), the app asks a server of ours, verificar.dgmstudio41003.com, whether the purchase is real. That server asks Google Play and answers yes or no. The app sends it only three things:

  • the purchase code given by Google Play, which identifies that purchase and not the person;
  • the product bought (the subscription or the one-time payment);
  • the name of the app.

Your email, your Google account, your measurements and anything you have typed are never sent.

The server does not store the purchase code: in its log it only records the date, the product, whether the purchase was valid and a summary of the code that cannot be used to look up the purchase. The connection goes through Cloudflare, which sees the IP address like any web server; the server uses it only in memory, to limit how many requests it receives per minute, and does not record it.

The answer is stored on your device, so that what you paid for keeps working without a connection for up to seven days.

The legal basis is the performance of the purchase contract and our legitimate interest in preventing fraud.

The files you create

When you export a pattern as a PDF or save a backup, the file goes wherever you choose on your device. From then on it is yours: if you share it, you are the one sharing it.

Deleting your data

What is on your device you delete yourself, whenever you want, from the app:

  1. Open Hilana and go to Settings.
  2. Tap Delete all my data and confirm.

Your measurements, the people, the swatches, the patterns and the notes are deleted, and the settings go back to how they were when the app was freshly installed, including what you answered about sending data. Uninstalling the app also deletes everything.

The phone's backup, if you have it turned on, is managed by your system. After you delete your data in Hilana, the next backup is made without it. To delete backups that have already been made, go to the backup settings of your Google account or of iCloud.

What was sent to Google Firebase, if you agreed (Android only):

  1. In Settings → Help improve the app, turn off both options. From that moment nothing more is sent, the crash reports waiting to be sent are deleted and the installation identifier is deleted, so that nothing sent in the future can be linked to what came before.
  2. What has already been sent is deleted on its own:
    • Usage statistics, two months after the last time you used the app.
    • Crash reports, after 90 days. After that, Google takes some time to remove them from its backups as well.

What was sent does not include your name, your email or anything you have typed, so we cannot tell which data is yours in order to delete it sooner. What does remain, without the identifier, are general counts of everyone at once (for example, how many sweater patterns were generated in a week), which do not reveal anything about anyone.

About the purchase: the server that checks it does not store anything that identifies you, not even the purchase code, so there is nothing of yours to delete there. The purchase itself is managed by Google Play, with your Google account, or by the App Store, with your Apple account.
